# Cover Letter — Referral Template Guide

## Purpose
A referral letter is a warm, personal ask. The goal is NOT to land the job directly — it's to
get a human champion inside the company who will forward your resume or put in a good word.
Keep it short, personal, and make the ask easy to say yes to.

## Tone
- Collegial and human — write like you're talking to a smart colleague, not a hiring committee
- Confident but not boastful — let the facts speak
- Specific — mention something real about the person's work or the company
- Easy ask — never put them in an awkward position

## Structure (3 paragraphs, ~150-200 words total)

### Para 1 — The Connection (~40 words)
Establish why you're reaching out to THIS person specifically:
- Mutual connection ("We both know [name]")
- Shared experience ("I saw your post about [topic] and it resonated")
- Admired work ("Your team's work on [product/feature] is something I reference often")
- Direct ask if no connection: "I know this is a cold ask, but I've admired [company]'s approach to X"

### Para 2 — The Pitch (~80 words)
2-3 sentences maximum. Pick the 1-2 most relevant things from Divakar's background:
- Lead with the most impressive brand + outcome
- Connect it directly to what the target company or role cares about
- Be specific with numbers where possible

**Good example:**
"I spent 5 years leading AI/ML product roadmaps at Meta (Marketplace Search) and Amazon (Search CX),
delivering $60M in search revenue across 15+ countries. I'm now building RAG LLM applications and
am drawn to [Company]'s focus on [specific AI/product initiative]."

### Para 3 — The Ask (~40 words)
Make it specific and easy:
- "Would you be open to a 15-minute call to share your experience at [Company]?"
- "If you think it might be a good fit, would you be comfortable forwarding my resume to the hiring team?"
- "I'd love your perspective on the team culture — would you have 10 minutes?"

## What NOT to do
- Don't attach the resume in the first message (offer to send it)
- Don't ask for a job directly ("Can you refer me for the PM role?") — too transactional
- Don't write more than 200 words
- Don't use corporate buzzwords ("synergy", "leverage", "passionate about")
- Don't apologize for reaching out ("Sorry to bother you...")
